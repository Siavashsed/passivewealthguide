## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it better to invest $75,000 in REITs or buy a rental property?

It depends on what you're optimizing for, but for most people with a full-time job, I think REITs are the better fit. I've owned both, so this comes from experience rather than theory.

Start with the rental. Leverage, tax advantages, and appreciation make it attractive, and a well-run property can outperform. But the spreadsheet version of a rental leaves things out. Repairs don't arrive on a schedule. In March 2021, a dead water heater in my Dayton duplex cost $1,850, and that one invoice took out about six months of the profit I'd expected. Add vacancy, insurance, property taxes, and the occasional emergency call, and the real return drops.

The biggest hidden cost is your time. If you value it at zero, rentals look great. If you value it at what you earn at work, the math changes quickly. Handling tenants, contractors, and surprise problems is a second job, even with a property manager, who takes a cut of the income.

REITs are the opposite. You give up some control and the upside from leverage, but you get liquidity, diversification across many properties, and almost no day-to-day work. You can buy or sell in minutes, and a single bad tenant doesn't hurt you. Dividends are taxed less favorably than some rental income, so a tax-advantaged account helps.

My rule of thumb: choose a rental if you enjoy the work, have cash reserves, and have the flexibility to handle problems. Choose REITs if you want real estate exposure without the second job.
