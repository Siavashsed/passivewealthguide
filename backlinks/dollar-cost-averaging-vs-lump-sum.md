## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it better to invest a lump sum all at once or use dollar-cost averaging?

For most people, historically, investing the lump sum all at once has been the better choice. But the right answer depends on more than the math.

The key finding comes from Vanguard's research, which compared investing a lump sum immediately against spreading it evenly over 12 months across US, UK and Australian markets. The lump sum came out ahead in roughly two-thirds of the periods tested. The reason is straightforward: markets rise more often than they fall, so money that's invested earlier has more time in the market, while money waiting to be invested earns cash-like returns.

That said, dollar-cost averaging (DCA) has real value in specific situations:

1. You'd panic if the market fell soon after investing. A strategy you can stick with beats a theoretically better one you abandon. If a 20% drop right after you invest would make you sell, phasing in reduces that risk.

2. The sum is large relative to your net worth. A windfall that represents most of your savings carries more emotional weight than a small top-up.

3. Valuations feel stretched and you want a psychological buffer. This isn't a prediction tool, but it can make the decision easier to live with.

I personally learned this the hard way. In March 2022 I received a $42,000 freelance payout and split it into twelve monthly purchases because the headlines scared me. Looking back, the lump sum would likely have served me better, but I understand why I chose what I did.

A practical middle ground: choose a short DCA window, such as three to six months, rather than a full year. You get some protection against regret without leaving cash idle for too long.
