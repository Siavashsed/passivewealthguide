I opened a Fidelity account one Tuesday night and moved to a three-fund portfolio: a total US stock fund, a total international stock fund, and a total bond fund. ## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is a three-fund portfolio, and is it enough for a long-term investor?

A three-fund portfolio is a simple way to own nearly the entire investable market with just three index funds: a total US stock fund, a total international stock fund, and a total bond fund. That's it. Between them you hold thousands of companies across dozens of countries, plus a bond cushion.

Is it enough? For most long-term investors, yes. The reasons are mostly about what you avoid rather than what you gain.

First, cost. Broad index funds charge very little, often a few hundredths of a percent a year. Compare that with a typical 1% advisory fee on top of fund costs. On a $140,000 balance, 1.1% is about $1,540 every year, and that drag compounds over decades.

Second, diversification. You don't need to pick sectors, themes, or the next hot fund. The total-market funds already hold them in proportion.

Third, behavior. The best portfolio is the one you keep through a crash. A simple structure is easier to understand, so you're less likely to panic and sell at the bottom.

The decisions that matter are your stock-to-bond split and your discipline. Choose a split you can stomach in a 30% drop, not the one that looks best in a spreadsheet. Then rebalance about once a year by bringing the funds back to your target percentages, and otherwise leave it alone.

What it won't do: it won't beat the market, and it won't protect you from a bad year. It also isn't a plan by itself, since you still need an emergency fund, sensible savings rates, and attention to taxes and account types.

I moved to this approach after seeing what I was paying an advisor in actual dollars. I wrote up the steps I used here, if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/passivewealthguide/three-fund-portfolio-low-fees/

For most people, simple and cheap beats clever and expensive.
