## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if a passive income opportunity is a scam in 2026?

The simplest test I know: if a pitch promises a fixed, high return with no real risk, it's a scam. Legitimate investments fluctuate. Anyone guaranteeing steady returns is selling something that doesn't exist.

I learned this the cheap way. In March 2024 I put $250 into a copy-trading app a stranger recommended under a YouTube video. After nine days the dashboard showed $311, and I felt clever. When I tried to withdraw, the excuses started: verification fees, minimum balances, delays. The profit was just a number on a screen.

Here's what I look for now:

1. Guaranteed or fixed returns. Real markets don't offer them, ever.
2. Fast, easy early gains. Scams often let you 'win' small to encourage a bigger deposit.
3. Withdrawal friction. Test a small withdrawal early. If it's difficult, stop and don't add more money.
4. Modern packaging. In 2026 scams rarely look sketchy. They use AI trading software language, professional dashboards, and 'done-for-you' business offers that look legitimate.
5. Pressure and urgency. 'Limited spots' or 'act today' is a sales tactic, not an investment feature.
6. Recommendations from strangers. Comment-section tips and unsolicited DMs are not due diligence.
7. Unverifiable operators. Check registration with a financial regulator, search the company name plus 'withdrawal' or 'complaints', and look for independent reviews rather than testimonials on their own site.

Real passive income exists, but it's usually slower, less exciting, and requires either capital or upfront work. Index funds, dividend stocks, rental property, and digital products all carry real risk and real timelines. If something sounds faster and safer than all of them, that's the warning.
