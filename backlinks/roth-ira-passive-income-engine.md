## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Can a Roth IRA really provide tax-free income for life, and what's the catch?

Yes, it can, and the catch is almost entirely about timing.

With a Roth IRA you contribute money you've already paid tax on. In return, qualified withdrawals in retirement, including all the growth, come out tax-free. There's no tax bill on the gains, and the account has no required minimum distributions during your lifetime, so you decide when and how much to take.

Here's the fine print that matters. A withdrawal of earnings is "qualified" only if you're at least 59½ and the account has been open for at least five years. Your original contributions are different. You can take those out at any time without tax or penalty, because you already paid tax on them. But if you pull out earnings early, you'll usually owe income tax plus a 10% penalty, with limited exceptions.

There are also annual contribution limits and income phase-outs. If your income is above the IRS threshold for the year, you may not be able to contribute directly, so check the current figures on IRS.gov before you plan around a number.

The real catch isn't a rule, though. It's behavior. The Roth works because of decades of compounding, and the biggest threat is withdrawing during a market drop or treating it like an emergency fund.

I opened mine in March 2016 with $500 and kept adding to it automatically. I've put in roughly $41,000 so far, and the lesson was how little the growth depended on anything clever. It depended on starting and not interrupting it.
